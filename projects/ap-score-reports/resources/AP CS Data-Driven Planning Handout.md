### **Instructions: From Data to Action**

Welcome to the Data Deep Dive\! This packet translates the numbers on your AP Instructional Planning Report (IPR) into actionable, classroom-level interventions. Please complete the following steps:

**Step 1: Analyze Your IPR Data**

Review your most recent IPR. Look specifically for areas where your students scored lower than the global/state average or below your own historical benchmarks.

**Step 2: Circle the Intersections (Target: 4 Areas)**

Turn to the matrix for your specific course (Section 1 for CSA, Section 2 for CSP). **Circle exactly 4 cells** that represent the intersection of your lowest-scoring Units/Big Ideas and Practices. If a circled cell contains an FRQ or Create Task label, use your FRQ/PT scores as additional evidence when deciding whether that intersection reflects a deeper instructional need.

* *Crucial Strategy:* **Prefer earlier units.** Computer Science is highly cumulative. If your data shows low scores in both Unit 1 and Unit 4, you should prioritize Unit 1\. Foundational gaps early on will prevent mastery of later, more complex topics.

**Step 3: Highlight Specific Skills**

Look closely at the detailed bullet points populated inside your 4 circled cells. **Highlight the specific skills, concepts, or tasks** within those cells that you believe are the primary roadblocks for your students. 

**Step 4: Build Your Action Plan**

Use the Concept Dependency Checks provided below the matrices to ensure you aren't missing prerequisite skills. Finally, fill out the Action Plan section at the bottom of the page to design your targeted instructional interventions.

### **Part B: CSA Concept Dependency Check**

*CS concepts are cumulative. Check for gaps in prerequisites before designing your intervention.*

* **Struggling with Data Collections (U4)?** Check mastery in *Selection & Iteration (U2)*. You cannot manipulate complex structures without a firm grasp of loops and conditionals.  
* **Struggling with Class Creation (U3)?** Check mastery in *Using Objects & Methods (U1)*. Students must understand how to *call* methods before they can *design* their own.  
* **Struggling with Analyze Code (P3)?** Check mastery in *Selection & Iteration (U2)*. Tracing execution requires rock-solid understanding of boolean logic and loops.
