# AP Score Reports as Instructional Data Collaborator Notes

## Working direction

This document captures collaborative notes for the fuller AP score-report analysis proposal. The main draft should remain polished and submission-ready; these notes are intended for brainstorming, feedback, and iteration.

## Core ideas to keep

- AP score reports are often reduced to averages and pass rates, which hide useful instructional patterns.
- Score-band exports can reveal patterns that are lost in aggregated class-level summaries.
- Benchmark comparisons can help teachers identify meaningful data gaps more precisely.
- Prerequisite concept mapping can expose root causes rather than just symptoms.
- The session should emphasize practical next steps for teachers, not just presentation of data.
- The tool and worksheet should feel accessible, teacher-friendly, and privacy-conscious.

## Feedback themes

### Strongest framing

- The big idea is compelling: moving from results to instructional decisions.
- The connection between AP data and classroom action is clear and useful.
- The use of both CSA and CSP examples gives the session broader relevance.

### Areas to sharpen

- Ensure the session does not feel too tool-heavy; the emphasis should remain on instructional decision-making.
- Consider balancing the demo of the tool with discussion and reflection.
- Clarify that this workflow is designed for practical classroom planning and AP Classroom implementation.
- Keep the narrative focused on teacher action: what to do next after analyzing the report.

## Open questions

- Should the session emphasize a single example or a comparison across two course contexts?
- Is the Data Wise language helpful or too formal for this audience?
- Would a stronger emphasis on intervention planning improve the proposal?
- Should the final version include more explicit language about using score reports to support student growth across the year?

## Possible revision ideas

- Add a short callout that the workflow can be used before or after AP Classroom data review.
- Consider a more direct sentence around diagnosing root causes and planning interventions.
- Make the practical tool-and-worksheet component feel like a concrete deliverable rather than an optional add-on.
- Use examples that connect to common teacher concerns, such as pacing, skill gaps, and exam readiness.

## Final note

The fuller proposal should feel like a teacher-driven, actionable workshop that helps attendees leave with a process they can use immediately.
