# AP Score Reports as Instructional Data

## Session Type

Session (1 hour)

## Abstract

AP Computer Science score reports offer valuable benchmarks, but turning them into instructional decisions takes a repeatable process. This 60-minute guided session lets teachers practice one in pairs or small groups. Each participant with College Board access works with their own Instructional Planning Report data, then regroups with others who share an action area. Participants without data join a group as supporters and take home a template for their own. Working through the Data Deep Dive worksheet, groups prepare score-band exports, compare results against benchmarks, diagnose likely gaps using the CSA and CSP Content & Skills Matrices and prerequisite dependencies, and draft an action plan. Groups finish by designing formative assessments to check whether the plan works across the next school year. The workflow follows Data Wise (Steps 2, 4, 6, and 7). Participants also learn about a free, privacy-focused browser tool that aggregates reports locally without uploading student information, so data stays on each participant's laptop. This session aligns with CSTA Teacher Standards 4f (plan instruction to foster student understanding), 4g (inform instruction through assessment), and 5f (guide students' use of feedback). It stands alone, and the companion mini-session explains the reasoning behind the process.

## Learning Goals

Participants will:

- Use score-band exports and benchmark comparisons to identify meaningful gaps in AP data.
- Diagnose likely root causes with the CSA or CSP Content & Skills Matrices and prerequisite dependency checks.
- Build an action plan that connects a targeted intervention to AP Classroom resources.
- Design formative checkpoints that show whether the plan is working across the next school year.
- Apply the process collaboratively in groups, with and without College Board data.

## Educational Basis

This session is grounded in four steps of the Data Wise Improvement Process (Harvard Graduate School of Education):

- Step 2, Build Assessment Literacy: participants learn to read score bands, benchmarks, and the effects of multiple test forms before drawing conclusions.
- Step 4, Dig into Student Data: participants analyze score-band and benchmark data to locate specific patterns and misconceptions.
- Step 6, Develop Action Plan: participants choose targeted interventions connected to AP Classroom resources.
- Step 7, Plan to Assess Progress: participants define formative indicators that show whether the plan is working.

Two research-based practices also shape the session: formative assessment, which uses classroom evidence to adjust instruction (Black and Wiliam, 1998), and concept-level diagnosis, since performance on specific concepts varies in how well it predicts programming ability (Cabo, 2015). The Step 7 formative assessment applies the first, and the Step 4 prerequisite checks apply the second.

## Standards Alignment

- **CSTA 4f, Plan instruction to foster student understanding:** Step 4 (0:11–0:31) and Step 6 (0:31–0:43). Prerequisite checks address misconceptions, and groups choose targeted interventions.
- **CSTA 4g, Inform instruction through assessment:** Steps 2, 4, and 6 (0:05–0:43). Groups use score-band and benchmark data to make instructional decisions and choose an intervention.
- **CSTA 5f, Guide students' use of feedback:** Step 7 (0:43–0:53). Groups build one formative assessment from the AP Classroom question bank, selecting questions tagged to the action area's content and skill.

## Session Outline

### 0:00-0:05 | Form Groups and Set Norms

- Groups form as participants arrive. Each participant confirms whether they have College Board access. Participants without access join a group as supporters.
- Norms: data stays on the laptop, and no student names leave the group.
- Engagement: groups assign roles (recorder, facilitator, timekeeper).

### 0:05-0:11 | Step 2: Build Assessment Literacy

- Review the worksheet glossary: score band, benchmark, and the effect of multiple test forms.
- Engagement: groups check the glossary terms against their own reports and note any they can't match.

### 0:11-0:31 | Step 4: Dig into Student Data

- Each participant with access sets up their own data slice, exports, imports into the browser tool, compares to benchmarks, and circles four low-performing cells on the Data Deep Dive worksheet (CSA or CSP matrix).
- Regroup: participants move to a group where everyone's data shows the same shared action area. Participants without data move with the group they were working with.
- In the new group, run the Concept Dependency Checks together.
- Engagement: each group names the shared action area and the skill it believes is the root cause.
- Discussion: Which part of your own AP data surprised you, and what would you need to check before believing it? Where did the prerequisite check change your view of the root cause?

### 0:31-0:43 | Step 6: Develop Action Plan

- Complete the Action Plan section: choose one targeted intervention for the shared action area and connect it to AP Classroom resources.
- Engagement: each group shares its intervention and the reason for it.
- Discussion: Why did your group choose this intervention over the others you considered?

### 0:43-0:53 | Step 7: Plan a Formative Assessment

- Build one formative assessment from the AP Classroom question bank, selecting questions tagged to the action area's content and skill. Define the indicator it will measure.
- Engagement: groups check each other's indicators for measurability.
- Discussion: What evidence would tell you by winter that the plan is working?

### 0:53-1:00 | Wrap-up and Resources

- Resources: Data Deep Dive worksheet with glossary, browser tool, and score-band export guide.
- Every participant takes home the action-plan template.
- Discussion: What would you need from your department to run this cycle with your own students?

## Discussion Questions

- Which part of your own AP data surprised you, and what would you need to check before believing it?
- Where did the prerequisite check change your view of the root cause?
- Why did your group choose this intervention over the others you considered?
- What evidence would tell you by winter that the plan is working?
- What would you need from your department to run this cycle with your own students?

## Participant Outcomes

Participants leave with a group action plan, a formative assessment for one action area, and a template for their own data. Tangible: the Data Deep Dive worksheet, a free privacy-focused browser tool, and a score-band export guide. Intangible: confidence to diagnose gaps with colleagues.
