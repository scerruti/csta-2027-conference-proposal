# AP Scores to Instructional Action: A Quick Workflow

## Session Type

Mini-Session (20 minutes)

## Abstract

Many AP Computer Science teachers can see where students struggled in their score reports. Finding the underlying cause and planning a response takes a repeatable process. This 20-minute mini-session explains why score-band exports, benchmark comparisons, and prerequisite analysis can reveal causes that averages and category summaries do not show. It introduces the Data Wise process for moving from results to action: building assessment literacy, digging into student data, developing an action plan, and planning formative checks for the next school year. Attendees leave with a take-home guide and a free, privacy-focused browser tool that aggregates reports locally without uploading student information. This session stands alone. The companion 60-minute session offers guided, partner-based practice with the same process. The session aligns with CSTA Teacher Standards 4f (plan instruction to foster student understanding), 4g (inform instruction through assessment), and 5f (guide students' use of feedback).

## Learning Goals

Participants will:

- Explain why score-band exports and prerequisite analysis can reveal causes that averages do not show.
- Describe how benchmark comparisons help confirm where a gap is real.
- Outline the Data Wise steps for turning AP results into an action plan with formative checks.
- Plan a first use of the take-home guide with their own AP data.

## Educational Basis

This session is grounded in four steps of the Data Wise Improvement Process (Harvard Graduate School of Education):

- Step 2, Build Assessment Literacy: participants learn to read score bands, benchmarks, and the effects of multiple test forms before drawing conclusions.
- Step 4, Dig into Student Data: participants analyze score-band and benchmark data to locate specific patterns and misconceptions.
- Step 6, Develop Action Plan: participants choose targeted interventions connected to AP Classroom resources.
- Step 7, Plan to Assess Progress: participants define formative indicators that show whether the plan is working.

Two research-based practices also shape the session: formative assessment, which uses classroom evidence to adjust instruction (Black and Wiliam, 1998), and concept-level diagnosis, since performance on specific concepts varies in how well it predicts programming ability (Cabo, 2015). The formative-checks segment applies the first, and the causes segment applies the second.

## Standards Alignment

- **CSTA 4f, Plan instruction to foster student understanding:** the causes segment (0:07–0:11). Prerequisite analysis helps find misconceptions and underlying gaps.
- **CSTA 4g, Inform instruction through assessment:** the score-band and benchmark segment (0:03–0:07) and the causes segment (0:07–0:11). Participants learn how data supports instructional decisions.
- **CSTA 5f, Guide students' use of feedback:** the Data Wise map (0:11–0:15) and the formative-checks segment (0:15–0:18), which shows how question-bank tags let a teacher build a formative assessment for an action area.

## Session Timed Outline

### 0:00-0:03 | Why Averages Stop Short

- Ask participants what they look at first when AP results arrive.
- Explain why averages and pass rates can hide where students struggle.
- Participant engagement: quick poll on what each participant currently looks at first.

### 0:03-0:07 | Why Score Bands and Benchmarks Matter

- Explain why mixed-performance groups can hide needs, and how multiple test forms affect comparisons.
- Participant engagement: predict whether a class-level or score-band view would show more.

### 0:07-0:11 | Why Causes Need a Second Look

- Explain prerequisite analysis conceptually, using one example from the Content & Skills Matrix as a picture, not a walkthrough.
- Participant engagement: name one suspected cause to test before choosing an intervention.

### 0:11-0:15 | The Data Wise Map

- Present the four steps this session uses: Build Assessment Literacy, Dig into Student Data, Develop Action Plan, and Plan to Assess Progress. Explain what each step answers.
- Participant engagement: put the four steps in order and say what each one answers.

### 0:15-0:18 | Where Formative Checks Fit

- Explain how the AP Classroom question bank's content and skill tags let a teacher build a formative assessment for an action area.
- Participant engagement: share one indicator they would use.

### 0:18-0:20 | Take-Home and Reflection

- Point to the take-home guide and browser tool, and to the 60-minute session for guided practice.
- In-session question: "Compared with how you use AP results now, are you data aware or data driven?"

## Participant Outcomes

Participants leave with a map of the Data Wise process for AP results and a take-home guide to start it with their own data. Tangible: the guide, a free privacy-focused browser tool, and formative-check planning steps. Intangible: confidence to test a suspected cause before choosing an intervention.

## Discussion Questions

In session (0:18–0:20):

- Compared with how you use AP results now, are you data aware or data driven?

Take-home prompts:

- Work through a CSA or CSP example showing multiple weak areas, using the Data Deep Dive worksheet.
- What patterns are hidden when we rely only on averages and pass rates?
- How can prerequisite concept analysis help us find the root cause of a student difficulty?
- What is one classroom move you would make after this analysis?

## Participant Resources

- **Take-home guide:** a one-page map of the four Data Wise steps with the question each one answers, a score-band checklist, a benchmark comparison sheet, prerequisite questions drawn from the Content & Skills Matrix, and a formative-checkpoint template (indicator, measure, and date).
- **Data Deep Dive worksheet:** the CSA and CSP Content & Skills Matrices, Concept Dependency Checks, and an Action Plan section.
- **AP Score Reports browser tool:** the same free, privacy-focused tool. It aggregates reports locally and never uploads student information. The companion 60-minute session offers guided practice with it.
