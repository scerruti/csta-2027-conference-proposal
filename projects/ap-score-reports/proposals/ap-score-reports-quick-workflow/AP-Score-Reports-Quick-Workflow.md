# From Data-Aware to Data-Driven: Acting on AP CS Score Reports

## Session Type

Mini-Session (20 minutes)

## Abstract

Knowing where students struggled on last year's AP exam is a start. Being data-driven means letting that knowledge change what you teach. This 20-minute mini-session gives AP CSA and CSP teachers a working definition of data-driven instruction (the data identifies where to look, the teacher's experience forms a hypothesis, and new data tests it) and a three-stage process for acting on AP score reports. Notice: read the score roster and turn what you see into a question. Investigate: slice the Instructional Planning Report to answer that question, combine its test forms with a free, privacy-focused browser tool, and treat the place where a weak unit and a weak practice meet as a hypothesis, prioritizing foundational concepts. Respond: plan an instructional change and build an AP Classroom formative assessment to test it during the year. A Think-Pair-Share in every stage has attendees apply each step to their own results, which are expected to arrive around conference time. Attendees leave with a slicing and download handout, the tool, CSA and CSP worksheets, and a guide to AP Classroom formative assessments. This session aligns with CSTA Teacher Standards 4g (inform instruction through assessment), 4f (plan instruction to foster student understanding), and 3f (participate in CS professional learning communities).

## Learning Goals

Participants will:

- Distinguish data-aware from data-driven instruction, using a working definition: the data identifies where to look, the teacher's experience forms a hypothesis, and new data tests it.
- Describe the three stages of the process (Notice, Investigate, Respond) and what each one produces.
- Explain why the process works: the question decides which data to examine, the weak area the report points to is a hypothesis rather than an answer, and a formative assessment tests that hypothesis during the year.
- Apply the first steps to their own context by drafting a question for their AP results and naming the slice that would answer it.
- Identify how ongoing collaboration with colleagues sustains the cycle after the session.

## Educational Basis

This session draws on four research-based practices:

- **Data use as a cycle** (Marsh, 2012). In a review of research on interventions that support educators' use of data, Marsh describes data use as a cycle. Data is organized into information, combined with educator expertise to become actionable knowledge, acted on, and evaluated. The session's three stages follow that cycle. Marsh found that educators most often lack support for moving from knowledge to action, which is the move from data-aware to data-driven that this session addresses. She also found that a single training session alone rarely led to data use, so the session closes with take-home resources and a case for ongoing collaboration with colleagues.
- **Formative assessment** (Black & Wiliam, 1998). Assessment is formative only when its evidence is used to adapt teaching. AP score reports arrive after the course ends; an AP Classroom formative assessment provides evidence during the year, while there is still time to respond. Here the assessment tests a hypothesis about one skill rather than serving as a grade.
- **Prioritizing foundational concepts** (Cabo, 2015). In a first college Java course, performance on foundational concepts such as assignment, repetition, and syntax predicted students' ability to write working programs, and a gap in any one concept held students back. AP CSA teaches the same language and many of the same concepts, so the session advises investigating earlier units first.
- **Think-Pair-Share** (Lyman, 1981). In each stage, every attendee thinks, then talks with a partner, before a whole-room question. A 20-minute overview still has each participant apply the process to their own results.

The collaborative step draws on two ideas from the Data Wise Improvement Process (Harvard Graduate School of Education): examining practice alongside evidence of student learning, and stating evidence before interpreting it.

## Standards Alignment

- **CSTA 4g, Inform instruction through assessment:** Notice and Investigate (0:02–0:12) show how AP results inform instructional decisions. Respond (0:12–0:18) shows how an AP Classroom formative assessment provides evidence to adjust instruction during the year.
- **CSTA 4f, Plan instruction to foster student understanding:** Investigate and Respond (0:06–0:18) locate the likely gap in understanding, prioritize foundational concepts, and plan the instructional change.
- **CSTA 3f, Participate in CS professional learning communities:** the Think-Pair-Share in each stage (0:02–0:18) has attendees share practice with peers, and the close (0:18–0:20) makes the case for continuing that work in a PLC or community of practice.

## Session Timed Outline

### 0:00-0:02 | Are You Data-Driven?

- Show of hands: "Are you data-driven?"
- Define it: the data identifies where to look, the teacher's experience forms a hypothesis, and new data tests it. By this definition, many teachers are data-aware: they know their results but haven't acted on them.
- Introduce the three stages: Notice, Investigate, Respond.

### 0:02-0:06 | Notice: Look, Then Ask

- Look: the Subject Score Roster shows the score distribution, mean, and percentage of scores 3 or higher, by section and against state and global peers.
- Ask: turn what you see into a question, such as "How do I get more students to pass?" or "How could my period 4 students earn a 5?"
- Engagement (Think-Pair-Share): "Think about the results you just received, or last year's. What question would you want them to answer?"
- Whole-room question: "Why start with a question instead of opening the detailed report?"

### 0:06-0:12 | Investigate: Slice, Combine, Hypothesize

- Slice: filter the Instructional Planning Report by score band and/or section to match the question, then download it. For example, look at the 2s, since 3 is passing.
- Combine: the report separates results by test form. A free, privacy-focused browser tool combines the forms into two tables, units and practices, without uploading any data.
- Hypothesize: where the weakest unit and practice meet on the Data Deep Dive matrix is a hypothesis, not an answer. Prefer earlier units, since later units build on them. Test the hypothesis against colleagues' experience: evidence first, then how each person taught it.
- College Board also recommends comparing two slices (such as 2s against 3s and above) as a next step to try on your own.
- Engagement (Think-Pair-Share): "Which slice would answer your question: which score band, which section?"
- Whole-room question: "The report shows content and skills separately. Why treat the place where they meet as a hypothesis rather than an answer?"

### 0:12-0:18 | Respond: Plan, Test, Adjust

- Plan: write an action plan for the hypothesis, fitted to your adopted curriculum.
- Test: build an AP Classroom formative assessment by filtering the question bank by learning objective, skill, or content area. Give it before and/or after the change. Its purpose is to test the hypothesis, not to serve as a grade.
- Adjust: when results come back, reteach or spiral, and carry the results into future planning. An assessment is formative only when its results change what you teach.
- Engagement (Think-Pair-Share): "What's one thing in your curriculum you'd change, and when in the year would you check on it?"
- Whole-room question: "Why give a formative assessment instead of waiting for next year's scores?"

### 0:18-0:20 | Close: Where You Go From Here

- Reflection: "By our definition, were you data-driven? What would it take to be?"
- The cycle works best with colleagues who revisit the plan and the results through the year, in a PLC, a community of practice, a CSTA chapter, or an online group. If your partner's question was close to yours, trade contact information.
- Point to the take-home resources.

## Participant Outcomes

Participants leave with a draft question for their own AP results and a map of the three-stage process. Tangible: slicing handout, browser tool, CSA and CSP worksheets, AP Classroom assessment handout. Intangible: confidence to act on results, not just read them.

## Discussion Questions

In session:

- Are you data-driven? (opening)
- Think about the results you just received, or last year's. What question would you want them to answer? (Notice, Think-Pair-Share)
- Why start with a question instead of opening the detailed report? (Notice, whole room)
- Which slice would answer your question: which score band, which section? (Investigate, Think-Pair-Share)
- The report shows content and skills separately. Why treat the place where they meet as a hypothesis rather than an answer? (Investigate, whole room)
- What's one thing in your curriculum you'd change, and when in the year would you check on it? (Respond, Think-Pair-Share)
- Why give a formative assessment instead of waiting for next year's scores? (Respond, whole room)
- By our definition, were you data-driven? What would it take to be? (close)

Take-home prompts:

- Your new results are arriving now. What is the first question you will ask of them?
- What did your 3s learn that your 2s did not? (College Board's recommended comparison of two slices)
- Who will you meet with when your formative results come back?

## Participant Resources

- **Slicing and download handout:** how to read the Subject Score Roster, turn what you see into a question (with sample questions and examples of questions the report can't answer), filter the Instructional Planning Report by score band and section to match the question, and download it.
- **Browser tool and documentation:** a free, privacy-focused tool that combines the report's test forms into two tables, units and practices, compared with global performance. Reports are processed in the browser and never uploaded. It works with the current report format.
- **Data Deep Dive worksheets, CSA and CSP versions:** each course's matrix of units and practices with sample skills in every cell, guidance on preferring earlier units, prerequisite checks, and an action-plan template.
- **AP Classroom formative assessment handout:** how to build an assessment by filtering the question bank by learning objective, skill, or content area; how to administer it; and how students and teachers see the results in AP Classroom.
