# CSTA 2027 Call for Proposals: Reviewers Rubric

Score each criterion from 1 to 5. Choose the highest tier whose full description the proposal meets. Base every judgment only on the proposal text and the attached Presentation Outline.

## Counting Rules

- **Practices:** count distinct practices, not steps, stages, or citations of one framework. Several steps from one framework (for example, four steps of Data Wise) count as one practice. A practice counts only if it is named in the proposal and grounded in research or sound pedagogy, which a citation or a clear rationale shows.
- **Resources:** judge a named resource by the proposal's description of it. Do not reward or penalize a resource for existing or not existing.
- **Standards:** count a standard only if the proposal names its indicator and ties it to a specific part of the session.

## 1. CSTA Standards (Teacher and/or Student Subtopics)

- 5: Existing CSTA Teacher Standards and Student Subtopics are identified, highly relevant, and thoroughly addressed.
- 4: Existing CSTA Teacher Standards and Student Subtopics are identified, mostly relevant, with minor areas for improvement.
- 3: Existing CSTA Teacher Standards and Student Subtopics are identified, but somewhat relevant, or only partially addressed.
- 2: Standards are identified but minimally developed or not relevant.
- 1: No existing CSTA Teacher Standards or Student Subtopics are identified, or the ones identified are not relevant.

## 2. Content (proposal outline)

Count how many of these four characteristics are clearly present:
- (a) Uses the given template (the CSTA 2027 Presentation Outline).
- (b) A detailed outline of learning goals, discussion questions, and activities.
- (c) Activities are appropriate for the proposal type (Workshop, Session, Mini, or Poster).
- (d) Activities can be achieved within the allotted time.

- 5: All four are clearly present.
- 4: At least three are clearly present.
- 3: At least two are clearly present.
- 2: At least one is clearly present.
- 1: None are clearly present.

## 3. Educational Best Practices / Pedagogy

- 5: Three or more educational and best practices are listed, each grounded in research or sound pedagogy and educationally robust.
- 4: Two or more are listed, each grounded in research or sound pedagogy and educationally robust.
- 3: One or more are listed, each grounded in research or sound pedagogy and educationally robust.
- 2: At least one is listed, with the same grounding requirement.
- 1: No educational and best practices are listed, or none are grounded in research or sound pedagogy.

## 4. Engaging and Relevance

Consider three things: how engaging the topic is, whether it fits the grade level(s) identified, and how creative, innovative, and/or relevant it is for CS education professionals.

- 5: Highly engaging, highly creative/innovative/relevant, and fits the grade level.
- 4: Engaging, creative/innovative/relevant, and fits the grade level.
- 3: Fairly engaging, fairly creative/innovative/relevant, and fits the grade level.
- 2: Somewhat engaging or somewhat creative/innovative/relevant.
- 1: Not engaging, or not creative/innovative/relevant, or does not fit the grade level.

## 5. Takeaways

- 5: Takeaways are extremely clear and include both tangible and intangible resources and objectives. Participant resources are detailed and describe handouts or other materials.
- 4: Takeaways are clear and include both tangible and intangible resources and objectives. Participant resources are detailed and describe handouts or other materials.
- 3: Takeaways are fairly clear and include some tangible and intangible resources. Participant resources lack detail and may not describe handouts.
- 2: Takeaways are somewhat clear, or include only tangible or only intangible resources.
- 1: No clear tangible or intangible takeaways. Participant resources do not describe handouts.

## 6. Attendee Preparedness

- 5: Attendees will feel extremely prepared to apply the skills and resources, and will be better computer science teachers.
- 4: Attendees will feel prepared to apply the skills and resources, and will be better computer science teachers.
- 3: Attendees will probably feel somewhat prepared to apply the skills and resources, and may be better computer science teachers.
- 2: Attendees will leave not feeling prepared to apply the skills and resources.
- 1: Attendees will not receive any skills or resources from the presentation.

## 7. Overall Recommendation

- 5: Strongly recommend acceptance (top 5% of submissions).
- 4: Recommend acceptance (top 25% of submissions).
- 3: Might recommend acceptance (top 50% of submissions).
- 2: Would not recommend acceptance.
- 1: Strongly recommend that the proposal be rejected.
