**Proposal:** AP Scores to Instructional Action: A Quick Workflow (Mini-Session, 20 min)

**1. CSTA Standards: 4/5 (Tier 4, "mostly relevant, with minor areas for improvement")**
- "CSTA 4g, Inform instruction through assessment: the score-band and benchmark segment (0:03–0:07)"
- "CSTA 4f, Plan instruction to foster student understanding: the causes segment (0:07–0:11)"
- "CSTA 5f, Guide students' use of feedback: the Data Wise map (0:11–0:15)"

All three standards name their indicator and point to a timed segment, so all three count. 4f and 4g fit well. The 5f link is weak: the segment is about teachers building assessments, not students using feedback. No Student Subtopics are named.

**2. Content: 5/5 (all four characteristics present)**
- "☒ Mini-Session - 20 minutes"
- "Engagement: put the four steps in order and say what each one answers."
- "Discussion: Compared with how you use AP results now, are you data aware or data driven?"

(a) The template is used. (b) Goals, discussion questions and activities are all laid out. (c) Short talks with quick engagement suit a mini-session. (d) The six segments add up to exactly 20 minutes. This is the top tier. One caution: the 3–4 minute segments are tight, since each pairs an explanation with an activity.

**3. Pedagogy: 5/5 (three or more grounded practices)**
- "four steps of the Data Wise Improvement Process (Harvard Graduate School of Education)"
- "formative assessment, which uses classroom evidence to adjust instruction (Black and Wiliam, 1998)"
- "concept-level diagnosis ... (Cabo, 2015)"

Under the counting rule, the four Data Wise steps count as one practice. With formative assessment and concept-level diagnosis that makes three, each with a citation or rationale. This is the top tier. One soft spot: the Cabo title is about Bloom's taxonomy categories, which fits loosely with the claim it supports.

**4. Engaging and Relevance: 4/5 (Tier 4)**
- "a free, privacy-focused browser tool that aggregates reports locally without uploading student information"
- "Participant engagement: quick poll on what each participant currently looks at first."
- "Many AP Computer Science teachers can see where students struggled in their score reports."

The topic is relevant and timely for AP CS teachers, and the privacy-first tool is a fresh angle. Most of the session is explanation ("Explain why..." in nearly every segment) with brief engagement moments, so it does not reach "highly engaging."

**5. Takeaways: 4/5 (Tier 4)**
- "a score-band checklist, a benchmark comparison sheet, prerequisite questions drawn from the Content & Skills Matrix, and a formative-checkpoint template (indicator, measure, and date)"
- "Intangible: confidence to test a suspected cause before choosing an intervention."
- "Data Deep Dive worksheet: the CSA and CSP Content & Skills Matrices, Concept Dependency Checks, and an Action Plan section."

The tangible resources are described in detail. The intangible side rests on one item, and the Participant Outcomes paragraph is brief, so the takeaways are clear but not "extremely clear."

**6. Attendee Preparedness: 3/5 (Tier 3, "probably feel somewhat prepared")**
- "using one example from the Content & Skills Matrix as a picture, not a walkthrough"
- "the 60-minute session for guided practice"
- "a take-home guide to start it with their own data"

The session is deliberately conceptual: attendees never practice the workflow or the tool, and hands-on work is pushed to the companion session. The guide helps, but without practice in the session, "feel prepared to apply" is not secure.

**7. Overall Recommendation: 4/5 (Recommend, top 25%)**
- "This session stands alone."
- "Black, P., & Wiliam, D. (1998). Inside the black box"
- "Plan to Assess Progress"

It is a tight, well-aligned, well-cited mini-session with useful resources. It falls short of the top 5% because of the lecture-heavy format, the weak 5f tie, and limited preparation for applying the workflow.

**Total: 29/35** (4 + 5 + 5 + 4 + 4 + 3 + 4)
