**Scores for "AP Score Reports as Instructional Data" (Session, 1 hour)**

**1. CSTA Standards: 4 (identified, mostly relevant, minor improvements)**
- "CSTA 4f, Plan instruction to foster student understanding: Step 4 (0:11–0:31) and Step 6 (0:31–0:43)."
- "CSTA 5f, Guide students' use of feedback: Step 7 (0:43–0:53)."
- Three indicators are named and tied to timed parts of the session. 4f and 4g fit well. 5f is a stretch, because Step 7 has teachers design a formative assessment for their own planning, not guide students' use of feedback. No Student Subtopics are named.

**2. Content (outline): 4 (tier 4, three or more characteristics clearly present)**
- Count: (a) template used, yes. (b) learning goals, discussion questions and activities, yes. (c) fits a Session, yes. (d) fits the time, not clearly.
- "0:11–0:31 Step 4: Dig into Student Data."
- "Build one formative assessment from the AP Classroom question bank" (10 minutes).
- The 20-minute Step 4 block holds data setup, export, import, benchmark comparison, regrouping and dependency checks. The 10-minute block asks for a built assessment and a defined indicator. Both look too tight to count (d) as clearly achievable.

**3. Pedagogy: 4 (tier 4)**
- "grounded in four steps of the Data Wise Improvement Process"
- "formative assessment, which uses classroom evidence to adjust instruction (Black and Wiliam, 1998)"
- "concept-level diagnosis ... (Cabo, 2015)"
- Counting by the rule, Data Wise's four steps are one practice. With formative assessment and concept-level diagnosis that makes three distinct named practices. Only formative assessment is solidly grounded. Data Wise is cited only by a web link under Related Links, and Cabo studied a first Java course (and Bloom's taxonomy), not AP data diagnosis. So I count two robust practices, with the third thin. Tier 5 needs three that are each educationally robust.

**4. Engaging and Relevance: 4 (tier 4)**
- "Participants without data join a group as supporters and take home a template for their own."
- "a free, privacy-focused browser tool that aggregates reports locally"
- "Which part of your own AP data surprised you"
- Using participants' own score data, the regrouping by shared action area and the privacy-first tool are relevant and practical. It fits AP CSA/CSP teachers. The topic is useful but not especially novel, and it serves only AP teachers.

**5. Takeaways: 4 (tier 4)**
- "Attendees take home all five resources, so they can repeat the process with their own data."
- "Score-band export guide: step-by-step instructions for setting up a data slice and exporting"
- "Intangible: confidence to diagnose gaps with colleagues."
- The five resources are described in detail. The intangible takeaway is a single phrase, so it is not "extremely clear".

**6. Attendee Preparedness: 4 (tier 4)**
- "Participants leave with a group action plan, a formative assessment for one action area, and a template for their own data."
- "What would you need from your department to run this cycle with your own students?"
- Attendees finish a full cycle and carry a reusable template. The schedule is tight, and supporters without data get less hands-on practice. That makes "extremely prepared" a stretch.

**7. Overall Recommendation: 4 (tier 4, top 25%)**
- It is coherent, practical and well aligned to the rubric. The tight timing, the loose 5f alignment and the thin grounding for the third practice keep it from the top 5%.
