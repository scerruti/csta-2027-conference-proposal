## 1. CSTA Standards
Quotes:
- "CSTA 4g, Inform instruction through assessment:** Notice and Investigate (0:03–0:26) use participants' own AP results to decide where to focus."
- "CSTA 4f, Plan instruction to foster student understanding:** Hypothesize (0:26–0:38) locates the likely gap in understanding and prioritizes foundational concepts, and Plan (0:38–0:44) designs the instructional change."
- "CSTA 3f, Participate in CS professional learning communities:** skill groups of teachers from different schools work together"

Why not the next tier up: Top tier. Three indicators are named and each is tied to timed parts of the session. All three are highly relevant to the activities, and no Student Subtopics are named, which the rubric's "and/or" allows.

## 2. Content
Quotes:
- "### 0:44-0:56 | Respond: Test" (the outline PDF uses the same time-stamped table of topics, discussion questions and engagement activities)
- "Discussion: Why give a formative assessment instead of waiting for next year's scores?"
- "Participants will: Distinguish data-aware from data-driven instruction"

Count: (a) present, since the PDF follows the CSTA 2027 Presentation Outline template with title, learning goals, type checkbox and a time/topics/discussion/engagement table. (b) present: six learning goals, five in-session discussion questions and timed activities. (c) present: a hands-on, small-group working session fits the 1-hour Session type. (d) present: the segments run 0:00 to 1:00 with no gaps, and each task is sized plausibly (slice and download 8 minutes, build the assessment 12 minutes). That is 4 of 4, so tier 5. The schedule is tight but credible.

Why not the next tier up: Top tier.

## 3. Educational Best Practices / Pedagogy
Quotes:
- "**Data use as a cycle** (Marsh, 2012)."
- "**Formative assessment** (Black & Wiliam, 1998). Assessment is formative only when its evidence is used to adapt teaching."
- "**Prioritizing foundational concepts** (Cabo, 2015)."

Count: (1) data use as a cycle, cited to Marsh 2012. (2) structured cross-school collaboration with norms, cited to Marsh 2012 and counted as a distinct practice. (3) formative assessment, cited to Black & Wiliam 1998. (4) prioritizing foundational concepts, cited to Cabo 2015, a first-college-Java study applied to AP CSA by stated rationale. Data Wise is a framework citation and counts as no extra practice. That is 4 distinct practices, each with a citation or clear rationale, so 3 or more is met and the tier is 5.

Why not the next tier up: Top tier.

## 4. Engaging and Relevance
Quotes:
- "In this hands-on session, AP CSA and CSP teachers work through a complete data-driven cycle with their own AP results."
- "Participants without data choose a group by interest."
- "By the end of this session you will have been data-driven with your own results."

Why not the next tier up: It is engaging, relevant and a good fit for the grade level, since AP is high school. The audience is narrow (AP CSA and CSP teachers) and the activity design is practical rather than strikingly innovative, so I did not rate it highly creative/innovative.

## 5. Takeaways
Quotes:
- "Tangible: slicing handout, browser tool, CSA and CSP worksheets, AP Classroom assessment handout. Intangible: confidence to repeat the cycle each year."
- "**Slicing and download handout:** how to read the Subject Score Roster, turn what you see into a question (with sample questions and examples of questions the report can't answer)"
- "**Data Deep Dive worksheets, CSA and CSP versions:** each course's matrix of units and practices with sample skills in every cell, guidance on preferring earlier units, prerequisite checks, and an action-plan template."

Why not the next tier up: Top tier. It gives clear tangible and intangible takeaways, and each of the four resources is described in detail.

## 6. Attendee Preparedness
Quotes:
- "Participants leave with a hypothesis drawn from their AP results, an action plan, and a scheduled AP Classroom formative assessment."
- "Participants use each resource during the session and take all four home to repeat the cycle each year."
- "If you can, bring a laptop and your College Board educator login; participants without access work alongside a partner."

Why not the next tier up: Attendees leave with finished work products, so they should feel prepared. "Extremely prepared" is less certain because some attendees will have no data or portal access and the pace is tight. The proposal also says the formative results only come back after the session.

## 7. Overall Recommendation
Quotes:
- "Being data-driven means the data identifies where to look, the teacher's experience forms a hypothesis, and new data tests it."
- "Marsh found that educators most often lack support for moving from knowledge to action, so the session's second half is devoted to planning and building an assessment."
- "The combined view is an approximation, good enough to decide where to look."

Why not the next tier up: It is a well-built, well-grounded and practical session that I would recommend. For top 5% it would need broader appeal or more innovation, because its value depends on a specific College Board report and tool and the audience is limited to AP teachers with access.

## Cannot be judged from the text
- Whether the browser tool, the handouts and the worksheets exist and work as described. I judged them only from the proposal's descriptions.
- Whether the College Board portal and report formats will be available and stable at conference time.
- How the room will actually manage logins, and how many attendees will have usable data.
- Presenter delivery quality and facilitation skill.
- How accurate the framing of the Cabo (2015) and Marsh (2012) findings is. I did not check the cited sources.
- How the proposal compares with the rest of the submission pool, which the top-5% and top-25% bands require.
