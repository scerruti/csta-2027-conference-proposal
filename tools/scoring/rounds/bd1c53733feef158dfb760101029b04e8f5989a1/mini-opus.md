## 1. CSTA Standards

Quotes:
- "CSTA 4g, Inform instruction through assessment: Notice and Investigate (0:02–0:12) show how AP results inform instructional decisions."
- "CSTA 4f, Plan instruction to foster student understanding: Investigate and Respond (0:06–0:18) locate the likely gap in understanding, prioritize foundational concepts, and plan the instructional change."
- "CSTA 3f, Participate in CS professional learning communities: the Think-Pair-Share in each stage (0:02–0:18) has attendees share practice with peers"

Count: three Teacher Standards (4g, 4f, 3f), each with its indicator named and tied to a timed segment, so all three count. No Student Subtopics are identified.

Why not the next tier up: 4g and 4f are highly relevant and thoroughly addressed. The 3f link is weaker: a Think-Pair-Share with a stranger at a conference is not taking part in a professional learning community, and the PLC itself is only recommended at the close. No Student Subtopics are named.

## 2. Content

Quotes:
- "☒ Mini-Session - 20 minutes"
- "Engagement (Think-Pair-Share): \"Which slice would answer your question: which score band, which section?\""
- "0:06–0:12 Investigate: Slice, Combine, Hypothesize."

Count:
- (a) Uses the CSTA 2027 Presentation Outline template: present. The PDF is the template, filled in with the type box checked.
- (b) Detailed outline of learning goals, discussion questions, and activities: present. There are five learning goals, eight in-session questions plus three take-home prompts, and activities in every timed block.
- (c) Activities appropriate for a Mini-Session: present. The activities are short Think-Pair-Shares and whole-room questions, which suit a 20-minute format.
- (d) Activities achievable within the allotted time: not clearly present. Each 4–6 minute block holds a content walkthrough, a full Think-Pair-Share and a whole-room question. Investigate, for example, packs Slice, Combine, Hypothesize, a colleague check, a pointer to College Board's two-slice comparison, a Think-Pair-Share and a whole-room question into 6 minutes.

Total: 3 of 4, which is tier 4.

Why not the next tier up: the timing is not clearly achievable. Three Think-Pair-Shares plus three whole-room discussions on top of content delivery in 20 minutes is very tight.

## 3. Educational Best Practices / Pedagogy

Quotes:
- "Data use as a cycle (Marsh, 2012). In a review of research on interventions that support educators' use of data, Marsh describes data use as a cycle."
- "Formative assessment (Black & Wiliam, 1998). Assessment is formative only when its evidence is used to adapt teaching."
- "Think-Pair-Share (Lyman, 1981). In each stage, every attendee thinks, then talks with a partner, before a whole-room question."

Count:
1. Data use as a cycle: cited (Marsh, 2012), with a rationale linking it to the three stages.
2. Formative assessment: cited (Black & Wiliam, 1998), with a rationale for testing during the year.
3. Prioritizing foundational concepts: cited (Cabo, 2015), with a rationale for how it carries over to AP CSA.
4. Think-Pair-Share: cited (Lyman, 1981), and used in every stage.

The two Data Wise ideas count as one framework under the counting rules. They are supplementary and are not needed to reach the count.

Total: 4 distinct grounded practices, so 3 or more, which is tier 5.

Why not the next tier up: Top tier.

## 4. Engaging and Relevance

Quotes:
- "A Think-Pair-Share in every stage has attendees apply each step to their own results, which are expected to arrive around conference time."
- "A free, privacy-focused browser tool combines the forms into two tables, units and practices, without uploading any data."
- "Hypothesize: where the weakest unit and practice meet on the Data Deep Dive matrix is a hypothesis, not an answer."

Why not the next tier up: the session is relevant and well timed for AP CSA/CSP teachers, fits that high school audience, and has some innovation (the browser tool, treating the weakest spot as a hypothesis). But the subject, reading AP score reports, is practical rather than highly engaging. It also serves a narrow slice of CS educators.

## 6. Attendee Preparedness

Quotes:
- "Apply the first steps to their own context by drafting a question for their AP results and naming the slice that would answer it."
- "Participants leave with a draft question for their own AP results and a map of the three-stage process."
- "She also found that a single training session alone rarely led to data use, so the session closes with take-home resources and a case for ongoing collaboration with colleagues."

Why not the next tier up: attendees practice only the first steps (a question and a slice) during the session. The Respond stage, building and using a formative assessment, is described but not practiced. The proposal itself admits that a single session rarely leads to data use, so "extremely prepared" is not supported.

- "Respond: plan an instructional change and build an AP Classroom formative assessment to test it during the year."
