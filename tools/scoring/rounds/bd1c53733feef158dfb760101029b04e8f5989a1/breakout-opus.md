## 1. CSTA Standards

Quotes:
- "CSTA 4g, Inform instruction through assessment: Notice and Investigate (0:03–0:26) use participants' own AP results to decide where to focus. Test (0:44–0:56) builds and schedules an AP Classroom formative assessment that provides evidence to adjust instruction during the year."
- "CSTA 4f, Plan instruction to foster student understanding: Hypothesize (0:26–0:38) locates the likely gap in understanding and prioritizes foundational concepts, and Plan (0:38–0:44) designs the instructional change."
- "CSTA 3f, Participate in CS professional learning communities: skill groups of teachers from different schools work together from the sort through Test (0:23–0:56)"

Why not the next tier up: Top tier. The proposal names three Teacher Standard indicators and ties each one to timed segments, so all three count. 4g and 4f are central to the session. 3f is the weakest of the three because the PLC work lasts only for the hour and the follow-up is optional. No Student Subtopics are named, but the rubric asks for Teacher and/or Student standards, so this does not lower the tier.

## 2. Content

Quotes:
- "Proposal Type: select one of the following ... ☒ Session - 1 hour" (outline PDF)
- "Discussion: Why start with a question instead of opening the detailed report?"
- "0:44–0:56 Respond: Test. Build an AP Classroom formative assessment by filtering the question bank by learning objective, skill, or content area."

Count:
- (a) Uses the template: present. The PDF follows the CSTA 2027 Presentation Outline, with the title, learning goals, type checkbox and time/topic table.
- (b) Detailed outline of learning goals, discussion questions and activities: present. There are six learning goals, five in-session discussion questions plus three take-home prompts, and engagement activities in each block.
- (c) Activities fit the proposal type: present. A hands-on, small-group session suits a 1-hour Session.
- (d) Achievable in the allotted time: present. The blocks add up to exactly 0:00–1:00, and the plan covers latecomers, people without access and crowded groups. It is tight, though: logging in takes 3 minutes, each of 3–4 group members gets about 3–4 minutes in the 12-minute hypothesis round, and building and scheduling the assessment takes 12 minutes.

Count: 4 of 4, which is tier 5.

Why not the next tier up: Top tier.

## 3. Educational Best Practices / Pedagogy

Quotes:
- "Data use as a cycle (Marsh, 2012). In a review of research on interventions that support educators' use of data, Marsh describes data use as a cycle."
- "Formative assessment (Black & Wiliam, 1998). Assessment is formative only when its evidence is used to adapt teaching."
- "Prioritizing foundational concepts (Cabo, 2015). In a first college Java course, performance on foundational concepts such as assignment, repetition, and syntax predicted students' ability to write working programs"

Count (distinct, named, grounded practices):
1. Data use as a cycle. Cited to Marsh 2012, with a rationale for giving the second half of the session to action.
2. Structured collaboration across schools, with norms that keep data discussions safe. Cited to Marsh 2012. This is a separate practice from the cycle, not one of its steps.
3. Formative assessment. Cited to Black & Wiliam 1998.
4. Prioritizing foundational concepts. Cited to Cabo 2015.

The two Data Wise ideas ("examining practice alongside evidence" and "stating evidence before interpreting it") are not counted separately. They come from one framework and support practice 2.

Count: 4 distinct grounded practices (three or more required), which is tier 5.

Why not the next tier up: Top tier.

## 4. Engaging and Relevance

Quotes:
- "In this hands-on session, AP CSA and CSP teachers work through a complete data-driven cycle with their own AP results."
- "Load the report into a free, privacy-focused browser tool. It combines the test forms into two tables, units and practices, compared with global performance; reports never leave the laptop."
- "Engagement: each group member leads the discussion of their own selection."

Why not the next tier up: Top tier. Teachers work with their own data and build a real artifact, and the browser tool is a new, privacy-focused idea. The grade level is clearly high school AP CSA/CSP. Two things limit it: it reaches fewer people because it is aimed only at AP teachers, and it depends on attendees bringing working College Board logins.

## 5. Takeaways

Quotes:
- "Tangible: slicing handout, browser tool, CSA and CSP worksheets, AP Classroom assessment handout. Intangible: confidence to repeat the cycle each year."
- "Data Deep Dive worksheets, CSA and CSP versions: each course's matrix of units and practices with sample skills in every cell, guidance on preferring earlier units, prerequisite checks, and an action-plan template."
- "Participants leave with a hypothesis drawn from their AP results, an action plan, and a scheduled AP Classroom formative assessment."

Why not the next tier up: Top tier. Both tangible and intangible takeaways are named, and each of the four handouts or tools has its own detailed description.

## 6. Attendee Preparedness

Quotes:
- "Each participant then writes an action plan and builds and schedules an AP Classroom formative assessment to test the hypothesis during the year."
- "Participants use each resource during the session and take all four home to repeat the cycle each year."
- "Adjust happens after today: when results come back, reteach or spiral, and carry the results into future planning."

Why not the next tier up: Top tier. Attendees run the whole cycle with their own data and leave with a scheduled assessment, so they should feel very ready to apply it. One limit: attendees without data access only shadow a partner or draft an assessment for the group's hypothesis, so they will be somewhat less prepared.

## 7. Overall Recommendation

Quotes:
- "By the end of this session you will have been data-driven with your own results."
- "Marsh found that educators most often lack support for moving from knowledge to action, so the session's second half is devoted to planning and building an assessment."
- "Results are expected to arrive around conference time, so the work starts with current data."

Why not the next tier up: Top tier. The proposal is tightly built and grounded in research. Its standards and timing match, its resources are concrete, and attendees produce something real during the session. The main risks are practical, not design flaws: login and portal access, a tight schedule, and an AP-only audience.

## Cannot be judged from the text
- Whether the browser tool actually works as described with the current report format, and whether it really keeps data local.
- Whether AP results and Instructional Planning Reports will be available at conference time ("expected to arrive around conference time").
- Whether venue Wi-Fi and College Board portal access will hold up for a room of simultaneous logins.
- The quality and accuracy of the handouts and worksheets beyond the proposal's descriptions.
- Whether the cited studies (especially Cabo 2015) support the claims as summarized.
- The presenter's delivery and facilitation skill.
- How this proposal ranks against the rest of the submission pool (needed for the top 5% / top 25% judgment).
