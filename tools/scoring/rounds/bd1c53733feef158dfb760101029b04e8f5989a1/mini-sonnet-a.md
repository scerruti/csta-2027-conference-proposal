## 1. CSTA Standards
Quotes:
- "CSTA 4g, Inform instruction through assessment: Notice and Investigate (0:02–0:12) show how AP results inform instructional decisions."
- "CSTA 4f, Plan instruction to foster student understanding: Investigate and Respond (0:06–0:18) locate the likely gap in understanding, prioritize foundational concepts, and plan the instructional change."
- "CSTA 3f, Participate in CS professional learning communities: the Think-Pair-Share in each stage (0:02–0:18) has attendees share practice with peers"

Why not the next tier up: Three teacher standards are named and tied to specific time segments, and 4g and 4f are highly relevant. The 3f tie is weaker, because paired Think-Pair-Share talk is only a thin form of PLC participation, and no student subtopics are named. That leaves minor areas for improvement.

## 2. Content
Count under the counting rules:
- (a) Uses the template: present. The PDF reproduces the CSTA 2027 Presentation Outline with title, learning goals, type checkbox and timed table.
- (b) Detailed outline of learning goals, discussion questions and activities: present. There are five goals, eight in-session questions and timed activities.
- (c) Appropriate for the proposal type: present. The 20-minute overview with a Think-Pair-Share in each stage fits a mini-session.
- (d) Achievable within the allotted time: not clearly present. The Investigate stage has 6 minutes for slice, combine and hypothesize, plus a Think-Pair-Share and a whole-room question. The Respond stage has 6 minutes for plan, test and adjust, plus the same two engagement items. Both look overpacked.
- Count is 3 of 4, which is tier 4.

Quotes:

- "0:12-0:18 | Respond: Plan, Test, Adjust"

## 3. Educational Best Practices / Pedagogy
Count under the counting rules:
1. Data use as a cycle (Marsh, 2012): named, cited and given a rationale.
2. Formative assessment (Black & Wiliam, 1998): named, cited and given a rationale.
3. Prioritizing foundational concepts (Cabo, 2015): named, cited and given a rationale. The research is a college Java course, but the proposal explains the transfer to AP CSA.
4. Think-Pair-Share (Lyman, 1981): named, cited and given a rationale.
- The Data Wise ideas are not counted separately, because they belong to one framework and are not a listed practice.
- Count is 4 distinct grounded practices, which is 3 or more.

Quotes:

Why not the next tier up: Top tier.

## 4. Engaging and Relevance

- "Show of hands: \"Are you data-driven?\""
- "which are expected to arrive around conference time"

Why not the next tier up: The topic is timely and practical, and attendees work on their own results. The approach is a sound process but not especially novel. Engagement is mostly Think-Pair-Share repeated in each stage. The fit is limited to AP CSA and CSP teachers, though that audience is clearly stated.

## 5. Takeaways
Quotes:
- "Tangible: slicing handout, browser tool, CSA and CSP worksheets, AP Classroom assessment handout. Intangible: confidence to act on results, not just read them."
- "Data Deep Dive worksheets, CSA and CSP versions: each course's matrix of units and practices with sample skills in every cell, guidance on preferring earlier units, prerequisite checks, and an action-plan template."
- "Participants leave with a draft question for their own AP results and a map of the three-stage process."

Why not the next tier up: The tangible resources are well described and there is an intangible takeaway. Only one intangible item is stated in the Participant Outcomes section (confidence), and nothing says how the materials are delivered to attendees. That is not quite "extremely clear".

## 6. Attendee Preparedness
Quotes:
- "Apply the first steps to their own context by drafting a question for their AP results and naming the slice that would answer it."
- "What's one thing in your curriculum you'd change, and when in the year would you check on it?"
- "Who will you meet with when your formative results come back?"

Why not the next tier up: Attendees leave with a process, a draft question and detailed worksheets, so they should feel prepared. In 20 minutes they will likely practice only the first steps and will not have actually built an assessment. Results may not have arrived yet, which limits how extremely prepared they will feel.

## 7. Overall Recommendation
Quotes:
- "Marsh found that educators most often lack support for moving from knowledge to action, which is the move from data-aware to data-driven that this session addresses."
- "Its purpose is to test the hypothesis, not to serve as a grade."
- "The cycle works best with colleagues who revisit the plan and the results through the year"

Why not the next tier up: The proposal is well grounded, well resourced and relevant, so I would recommend it for acceptance. It falls short of top 5% because of the tight timing, the narrow AP-only audience, and the modest novelty.
